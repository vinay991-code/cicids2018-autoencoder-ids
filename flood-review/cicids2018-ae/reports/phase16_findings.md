# Phase 16 - Experiment G: label-free, rate-limited adaptive threshold on a frozen autoencoder (measured results)

Code: `src/phase16_adaptive_threshold.py` (experiment and checks), `src/summarise_threshold.py` (tables and figure). Full tables: `results_threshold/threshold_summary.md`; data: `results_threshold/phase16_adaptive_threshold.json` and `phase16_intervals.csv`; figure: `results_threshold/phase16_overview.png`. One run (the first), seed 42 baseline of Phase 7, settings fixed in the script header before any arm was evaluated. The arms are deterministic: a second run after adding the post-hoc grouping below reproduced the same numbers.

## What this experiment is, and what it is not
- **Threshold adaptation only.** The autoencoder is frozen. Every record has the same score in every arm, so ROC-AUC and PR-AUC are identical in all arms and the ranking of attacks against benign records cannot improve. Only the decision point moves.
- **Not model adaptation.** Phase 7 arm C, Phase 8 and Phase 9 retrain the network; this does not.
- **Not concept-drift recovery.** A model whose ranking is inverted on a shifted day stays inverted. The frozen model has ROC-AUC 0.079 on 02-16 and 0.013 on 02-21, below chance; no threshold can detect attacks that score lower than benign records.
- The threshold policy sees only past and current reconstruction scores (`policy_trace` has no label argument). Labels are read afterwards, to count results and for diagnostics that are marked as such.

## Setup
- **Stream, baseline and intervals: exactly those of Phase 7.** One-class autoencoder trained on the benign records of days 02-14 and 02-15; initial threshold theta0 = 0.013937 (99th percentile of later benign warm-up errors); evaluation stream = the 9,504,036 records of the 8 following days (1,158,860 attacks, 12.19%) in 194 intervals of at most 50,000 records. Each interval is flagged with the threshold computed before it and then used to update the threshold.
- **Reproduction checks (all pass).** The numpy forward pass reproduces the stored warm-up threshold exactly; the intervals equal Phase 7's; the static counts equal Phase 7 arm A except for a total absolute difference of 6 over 776 count cells (records within float rounding of the threshold); per-day ROC-AUC equals Phase 7's static values; rate limit and clamp are respected; the headline numbers were recounted independently from `phase16_intervals.csv`.
- **Arms.** **S** static. **D** adaptive: window of the last 6 intervals (about 300,000 scores); statistic = lower quartile of the window's log10 scores plus a constant offset of 3.157 decades measured on warm-up benign scores (so that it reproduces theta0 on warm-up-like traffic); at most 0.25 decades (a factor 1.78) of change per interval; threshold kept within theta0 / 10 and theta0 x 1000. **D1** = D without the rate limit. **D2** = the Phase 7 B statistic (median + k x MAD, k = 4.286, all records of the window) with D's rate limit and clamp. Phase 7 arms B (same statistic, no rate limit, 10,000 sampled records per interval) and C (retrained model) are quoted from `results/phase7_main.json`, not re-run.
- **Safeguards against anomalous records** (D): the lower quartile ignores the highest-scoring three quarters of the window, so attack campaigns that score high cannot pull it up; the rate limit stops a single odd interval from moving the threshold far; the clamp bounds it.

## Findings
1. **The adaptive threshold traded detection for fewer false alarms and did not improve F1.** False alarms fell from 101,420 to 4,644 (FPR 0.0122 to 0.0006), precision rose from 0.135 to 0.434 (the interval of the difference includes 0), but recall fell from 0.0136 to 0.0031 and F1 from 0.0247 to 0.0061 (lower; the interval of the difference, -0.0434 to -0.0004, only just excludes 0). The Matthews correlation coefficient of the pooled counts is higher for D (0.0280 against 0.0043, no interval computed); both are close to zero, so neither detector is useful at this operating point. The static detector was already weak (recall 1.4%).
2. **D stopped alarming altogether for a third of the stream.** 64 of 194 intervals had no alarm at all with D, against 7 with the static threshold (56 with D1). The threshold was above everything in the interval.
3. **Why: the rule assumes the shape of the benign score distribution stays fixed.** The offset ties the threshold to the lower quartile. On 02-16 the benign median score was 253 times the warm-up median, but the benign 99th percentile was only 1.9 times theta0 (0.0271 against 0.0139). The bulk moved and the tail did not, so D raised the threshold to 6.39, far above the interval's benign 99th percentile (median over the day 0.0245). On 02-21 the benign median was 61 times the warm-up median while the benign 99th percentile was 0.0018, and D went up to 2.01. A threshold that assumes a fixed shape cannot be right when the shape changes.
4. **Shifted traffic periods.** The rule declared in advance (static FPR above 0.5 on the day) matches no day of this stream: with the p99 threshold of Phase 7 the static detector's FPR on 02-16 and 02-21 is 0.0457 and 0.0001 (in Phases 8 and 9, with their anchor-based threshold, it flagged 99.6% and 99.9% of benign records on those days). After seeing this, a **post-hoc, descriptive** grouping was added (labels used; a day is "bulk-shifted" if its benign median score is at least 10 times the warm-up median): 02-16 and 02-21. On those days S has precision 0.386, recall 0.037, F1 0.068, FPR 0.0253; D has 0.852, 0.0057, 0.0113 and 0.0004. On the other six days S has F1 0.0065 and FPR 0.0107, D has 0.0039 and 0.0006. In every group D lowers the false alarms and loses recall.
5. **The safeguard against attack campaigns worked where it was tested, but not against benign shifts.** On 02-20, in the intervals where about half of the records were attacks (intervals 34 to 55 of the stream, 575,364 attacks on the day), the D threshold moved only from 0.027 to about 0.049, while D2, which uses the median and MAD of all scores, climbed in 10 steps to its ceiling of 13.9 and stayed there for 12 intervals (figure). The lower quartile resists a high-scoring campaign, as designed, but a benign shift moves it just as it moves the benign bulk (finding 3).
6. **The rate limit changed little.** D and D1 have the same F1 and FPR. The limit slows the return of the threshold after a shift: the threshold peaks at 6.39 at the end of 02-16 and is still above 0.5 at the start of 02-20, because 2.4 decades at 0.25 decades per interval take about 10 intervals; D1 had 56 intervals without any alarm against 64 for D.
7. **Relation to the earlier negative results.** Phase 7 B (an adaptive threshold from all records) was already worse than static (F1 0.0074). D2, which is B with a rate limit, is no better (0.0077); D, which adds a robust statistic, has a higher precision and a lower F1. None of the threshold-only arms comes near the retrained model's ranking quality on shifted days (Phase 7: ROC-AUC 0.472 and 0.745 on 02-16 and 02-21 for the retrained model against 0.079 and 0.013 for the frozen one), which is something thresholds cannot change.

## Limitations
- One stream, one frozen model (seed 42), one family of rules. The settings were fixed in advance and not tuned; a different statistic (for example one that estimates the upper tail directly instead of the lower quartile plus a fixed offset) could behave differently. This does not show that no threshold-adaptation scheme can work, and I did not test other schemes.
- The grouping into bulk-shifted days is post-hoc and uses labels; it describes the data and was not chosen by looking at any arm's result, but it was added after the declared rule matched nothing.
- All F1 values are below 0.03. The intervals of the differences exclude 0 for F1, recall and FPR (F1 and recall only just), but they are differences between very weak detectors. 02-22 and 02-23 contain only 319 and 535 attacks, so their per-day recall and precision are not meaningful.
- The bootstrap describes sampling variation over the stream (moving blocks of 8 intervals), not variation between model trainings; there is one training run.
- The oracle benign 99th percentile used in the figure and in Table 5 uses labels and is a reference, not a target the detector could use.
- The policy keeps its state (last 6 intervals of scores and the current threshold), so it can run online; it was evaluated here on the recorded stream, not in the dashboard.
