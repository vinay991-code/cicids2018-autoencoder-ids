# Novelty audit of the CICIDS2018 experiment, finding by finding

**Date:** 13 November 2026. **Scope:** only the experiment already run in this project (supervised and unsupervised autoencoders on CSE-CIC-IDS2018, with the follow-up experiments). No new dataset, method or study is proposed here.
**Method:** for each finding, the measured evidence (with its place in the report), a targeted literature check, and how well the sources could be verified. A rating of *candidate* means "no paper was found that reports this", **not** that none exists: the search was web-based, and several sources could not be read (section 4).
**Ratings:** *Known* (published, read), *Partial* (published in part or with a different protocol or metric), *Candidate* (not found in the sources checked), *Not assessed* (sources unread).

## 2. Where the novelty most plausibly is

**Strongest: F2 together with F3 and F1, one story.** *In contaminated NIDS training data, the highest-loss records are mostly benign, so loss-based cleaning cannot work; a latent regulariser changes the threshold behaviour but not the ranking.* Support: three seeds, three trimming levels, a diagnostic that uses labels only for reporting, four lambdas with a benign-only control, independent verification. Honest weaknesses: one dataset; DAE-LR is re-implemented from an equation; the mechanism is plausible from general knowledge about autoencoders (the unread ICS paper may state a version of it); a negative result suits a workshop or a negative-results track better than a main track.

**Supporting: F4** (threshold versus ranking, with the exact-prevalence result), **F5** (strict unseen-attack protocol) and **F6** (ordinary-traffic false alarms) as evidence that the same detectors behave very differently depending on training-data purity and threshold choice. Each overlaps published work in part, so none should carry a headline claim.

**Not a headline:** F1, F9 (known), F7 (unassessed), F8 (documentation).

## 3. Cheap analyses on data already held (no new dataset)

1. **Labelled-threshold F1 of all models, as in the DAE-LR paper (`src/phase15_labelled_threshold.py`).** Uses saved scores; a labelled 20% of the test set picks the F1-maximising threshold, the other 80% is scored, in two versions (random rows, and whole 60 s blocks, which avoids neighbouring near-duplicates). Tests directly whether the published DAE-LR gain is a threshold effect. **Reporting only; not label-free.** Result: done, see section 5 (no DAE-LR gain; contaminated models at the flag-everything floor).
2. **Per-epoch loss-ranking diagnostic for F2:** during training, record the attack share among the highest-loss rows of every epoch for the ordinary and the trimmed models. This would show when and why attacks stop being high-loss. It needs retraining with logging (about 6 minutes per model).
3. **More seeds for Phase 12** (F4 rests on one trained model per setting).
4. **Per-attack-type view of contamination** (which types get learned as normal), from saved scores.

## 5. Result of the labelled-threshold re-evaluation

Script `src/phase15_labelled_threshold.py`, aggregation and an independent recomputation with scikit-learn's precision-recall curve in `src/aggregate_labelled.py` (12 of 12 checks passed); numbers in `results_labelled/labelled_summary.md`. 20 random splits per seed, 3 seeds; **the threshold uses test labels, so this is not a label-free result.** Test attack share 0.1193, so flagging everything gives F1 = 0.213.

| Model (3 seeds) | F1, random-row split | F1, block split |
|---|---|---|
| Unsupervised, ordinary loss | 0.229 | 0.196 |
| Trimmed loss 10 / 20 / 30% | 0.233 / 0.222 / 0.227 | 0.206 / 0.205 / 0.206 |
| DAE-LR on mixed data, lambda 0.01 to 10 | 0.226 to 0.235 | 0.193 to 0.214 |
| Benign-only, ordinary loss | 0.713 | 0.644 |
| DAE-LR benign-only, lambda 0.01 / 0.1 / 1 / 10 | 0.692 / 0.630 / 0.623 / 0.564 | 0.631 / 0.587 / 0.565 / 0.507 |

**What it shows.**
1. **DAE-LR does not help under the labelled-threshold protocol either.** Its F1 on mixed data (0.226 to 0.235) is the same as the ordinary model's (0.229), so the missing gain is not an artefact of my ranking metric: no gain appears under either evaluation. (My implementation follows only the published equation, and the data splits differ from theirs.)
2. **All contaminated models sit at the trivial floor.** Their best F1 equals about that of flagging everything (0.213), and their false-positive rates at the chosen threshold are 0.69 to 0.82 across all 8 contaminated models (block split): the F1-maximising threshold flags most of the traffic. F1 at a labelled threshold therefore cannot distinguish methods that rank at chance from one another. **Worth checking in the DAE-LR paper:** whether its reported F1 values lie above the flag-everything F1 of its own test prevalence (that needs its results table and prevalence, which could not be read).
3. **Row splits are optimistic.** For the benign-only model the random-row split gives 0.713 against 0.644 with whole blocks, which supports splitting by block when labelled thresholds are used.
4. **The latent term costs ranking on clean data** (0.631 falling to 0.507 in F1 as lambda grows from 0.01 to 10), the same direction as in Table 8b.

## 6. What reading the full texts changed (13 November, later)

Read from PDFs: Nkashama et al. 2022 (arXiv 2207.03576) and 2024 (arXiv 2407.08838), RCA (IJCAI 2021), Engelen et al. 2021 (flow construction section), Perini et al. 2022 (TRADE), TII-SSRC-23 (arXiv 2310.10661).

**Confirmed**
- Nkashama 2022, Table 2, CSE-CIC-IDS2018: autoencoder F1 63.0 at 0% and 42.0 at 12% contamination, DUAD 67.5 and 43.7; thresholds chosen on a labelled 20% of the test set; no ROC-AUC. This is what the report's Chapter 2 says.
- Nkashama 2024 gives no network, optimiser or lambda for DAE-LR (searched the full text); the centre c is a learnable parameter; the score is the reconstruction error. This is what the report says.
- RCA (IJCAI 2021): 18 benchmark datasets from the ODDS library (vowels, pima, optdigits, cardio, thyroid and so on), none an intrusion dataset; selection by lowest reconstruction error per mini-batch; the guarantees are theorems under convexity assumptions and AUC results; it does not measure the attack share among selected samples on intrusion data. So F2 stays a candidate.
- TII-SSRC-23, Table IV (now read in the paper): Isolation Forest AUROC 58.21, F1 1.54 at the 99th percentile and 0.0 at the maximum threshold; the paper says the results "emphasize the significant effect of threshold selection". F4's literature status stands (threshold sensitivity at fixed AUROC is published; the exact-prevalence test was not found).
- Engelen et al. 2021 state in the paper that the tool ended a flow at the first FIN packet, producing "TCP appendices" of leftover ACK and FIN packets, 25.9% of CICIDS2017. This matches the rule found in the CICIDS2018 training data (flows that start with a FIN have 2 packets, 6.0% of TCP flows) and is cited in the report.

**New observations (leads, not conclusions)**
1. **The same numbers appear under different labels.** In the 2022 paper the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 rows labelled DAE (precision 69.5, recall 57.6, F1 63.0 at 0%; 43.2, 41.6, 42.0 at 12%) and DUAD (62.2, 73.8, 67.5; 41.2, 46.9, 43.7) have the same values as the 2024 paper's DAE-LR and DUAD-LR rows. For DAE the match includes the standard deviations (at 0% and at 12%); for DUAD the means match at both levels and the standard deviations match at 0%, while at 12% they differ (2022: 1.9, 7.2, 4.1; 2024: 0.9, 0.2, 0.1). The 2024 paper's own plain DAE row is different (60.2 at 0%, 23.4 at 12%). The 2024 improvement of DAE-LR on this dataset (23.4 to 42.0 at 12%) is therefore measured against a lower baseline than the one the same group reported in 2022. The texts do not explain this. It is a reason for care when quoting the improvement, and a question to put to the authors, not an accusation.
2. **A trivial floor for their F1 values (inference).** From the 2022 description of the split (half of the normal data for training, the rest of the normal data plus a share 1 - gamma of the attacks for testing; 12% contamination needs 12/88 of the 6.74 million training normal rows, about 0.92 million attacks, which only fits the contamination set if gamma = 40%), the test attack share would be about 20%, so flagging everything would score an F1 of about 0.33. Their plain autoencoder at 12% (23.4), DAGMM (15.9), NeuTraLAD (24.8) and DSEBM (31.8) would then be below that, and DAE-LR (42.0) and DUAD-LR (43.7) a little above. This depends on the unstated gamma and cleaning, so it is a lead; the paper does not report a flag-everything baseline.
3. **Consistency with this project.** Under the labelled-threshold protocol on the CICIDS2018 test set of this project, all contaminated models, DAE-LR included, sit at the flag-everything F1 (section 5). The literature numbers above suggest the same kind of floor effect, in a different split.

**Rating changes:** none upward or downward. F3 keeps "candidate" and now rests on a fully read source. Sources still unread: the Mathematics 2023 ICS paper, the Flenman thesis, the MDPI per-class paper, ADAPT-IDS, the Politecnico thesis, the full text of Liu et al. 2022.
