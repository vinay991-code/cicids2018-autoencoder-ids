Adaptive baseline (Phase 7)

    

    
How it works
      
The stream is cut into intervals. Every interval is scored and flagged with the model and threshold the arm already has; only afterwards does the arm update itself from that interval, so nothing is used before its predictions are made.

      
A keeps one model and one threshold. B keeps the model but re-estimates the threshold every interval from the recent scores of all records (a robust median + k x MAD rule; k is chosen so the starting threshold equals A's). C uses B's threshold and also fine-tunes the autoencoder on a rolling trusted buffer: from every interval a random sample is drawn and only records that the arm itself scored as normal enter the buffer. Guards: a cap per interval, replay of clean warm-up records, and an update is rejected if the error on a fixed clean anchor set grows too much. No attack label controls any update.

Concept drift monitoring (Phase 9)

    

    
How it works
      
A sentinel model (the warm-up autoencoder, never updated) scores every record, so adapting any arm cannot feed back into detection. The stream is cut into small batches and each batch gives a few statistics: the mean log reconstruction error, the share of records the sentinel flags, the means of five traffic features and the UDP share. Each statistic feeds its own ADWIN change detector; an alarm is raised when any detector reports a change.

      
The arms differ in what they do with an alarm: A ignores it and never updates, A2 retrains on a fixed schedule without looking at alarms, B retrains after an alarm, C acts on an alarm only if the deviation persists in the following intervals and leaves the highest-scoring records out of the retraining data. Detector settings are fixed in advance and no label controls them.
